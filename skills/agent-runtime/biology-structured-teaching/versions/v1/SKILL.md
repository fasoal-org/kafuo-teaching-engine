# Biology Structured Teaching

Purpose: accurate biological understanding through organized concepts, structures, functions, processes, relationships and scientific classification.

## How this Skill works in the lesson

- This is the PRIMARY Skill of every teaching slide at this stage. Its subject method leads how the slide teaches. A pedagogical Skill (UbD, Feynman and the like) may support it, never replace it.
- The Teaching Model Flow instructions and the slide's role (explanation, worked_example, check_understanding, summary, transition) decide which slides exist, what each slide covers, and their order. Never add, merge, split or reorder slides because of this Skill.
- The grounded textbook content is the authority for facts, definitions, notation, examples and answers. That means the cited Content Units and their book kinds: key concept, worked example, check, prior knowledge, exercise. Apply the method below only where that content supports it.
- Follow the lesson's language directive and narration rules for the spoken register.

## Shared principles

Academic accuracy
- Follow the approved curriculum content and this slide's learning objective.
- Keep definitions, terminology, notation, formulas and relationships exactly as the textbook states them.
- Never invent facts, data, examples or results and present them as textbook content. An illustration of your own must be correct and must not contradict the book.

Clear explanation
- Say what an idea means and why it matters before using it.
- Introduce an unfamiliar term or symbol before relying on it.
- Connect the step to the prerequisite it needs.
- No repetition, no filler.

Progressive teaching
- Move from the foundation to the application.
- Justify each important reasoning step instead of stating an answer.
- Use a visual only when it makes a relationship, process, structure or comparison clearer.

Worked examples
- Name the given information and what is required.
- Say why the chosen method fits.
- Work in meaningful steps.
- Check the result when a check is possible.

Common mistakes
- Address a mistake only when it is real and relevant to this content.
- Say why it is wrong and contrast it with the correct reasoning, at the point where it would happen.
- Never invent a misconception to fill a slot.

Independent understanding
- Link the explanation to the lesson's own checks and practice.
- Never reveal answers to the quiz or question-bank items the learner answers alone.
- A solution the Teaching Model Flow asks for on this slide is part of teaching, and you write it in full. An example is the solution slide for a «تحقق من فهمك» item.

## Biology method

1. **Prerequisites.** Connect the new lesson to the biological knowledge it builds on, such as the cell, the organ system or the earlier process it depends on.
2. **The concept.** Introduce the main biological concept with accurate scientific terminology, as the textbook names it.
3. **Wide view first.** When it helps, start from the wider system or concept, then explain the detailed components: from organism to organ to tissue to cell to structure.
4. **Structures.**
   - Identify the structure.
   - Describe its important characteristics.
   - Explain its function.
   - Link each characteristic to the function it serves, for example "the folded inner membrane gives a larger surface for…".
5. **Processes.**
   - Explain the purpose of the process, or its biological significance.
   - Present the stages in their logical sequence.
   - Explain how each stage leads to the next.
   - Name the inputs, outputs and controlling conditions, such as enzymes, temperature, light or hormones.
6. **Classification.**
   - Identify the classification criteria.
   - Explain the defining characteristics of each group.
   - Compare related groups.
   - Highlight the distinguishing features, and the exceptions the curriculum supports.
7. **Representations.** Use labelled diagrams, process illustrations, classification tables and concept relationships. Every label uses the textbook's term.
8. **Connections.** Link related concepts, such as structure to function, a process to the organ that performs it, or a group to its characteristics, instead of presenting isolated facts.
9. **Misconceptions.** Explain the relevant ones, especially confusion between:
   - two structures, or a structure and its function;
   - two processes, such as respiration and breathing, or photosynthesis and respiration;
   - two taxonomic categories.
10. **Active recall.** Support meaningful recall through the lesson's existing checks, practice and assessment, for example by ending a slide with the relationship the learner should be able to state.

## By slide role

- **explanation:** the concept in context, then the structure–function or stage-by-stage explanation, with a labelled visual.
- **worked_example:** for an applied question, given → required → the biological principle and why it applies → reasoning steps → conclusion.
- **check_understanding:** set the items clearly. Give no answers unless the flow instructions ask for them.
- **summary:** the key relationships (structure–function, stage order, classification criteria), plus the one confusion most worth avoiding.

Do not force a process-based explanation onto a lesson that is only about structures or classification.
