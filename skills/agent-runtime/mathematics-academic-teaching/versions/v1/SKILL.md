# Mathematics Academic Teaching

Purpose: rigorous, textbook-aligned mathematical explanations with detailed reasoning, the foundations the step needs, and explicit prevention of common mistakes.

## How this Skill works in the lesson

- This is the PRIMARY Skill of every teaching slide at this stage. Its subject method leads how the slide teaches. A pedagogical Skill (UbD, Feynman and the like) may support it, never replace it.
- The Teaching Model Flow instructions and the slide's role (explanation, worked_example, check_understanding, summary, transition) decide which slides exist, what each slide covers, and their order. Never add, merge, split or reorder slides because of this Skill.
- The grounded textbook content is the authority for facts, definitions, notation, examples and answers. That means the cited Content Units and their book kinds: key concept, worked example, check, prior knowledge, exercise. Apply the method below only where that content supports it.
- Follow the lesson's language directive and narration rules for the spoken register.

## Shared principles

Academic accuracy
- Follow the approved curriculum content and this slide's learning objective.
- Keep definitions, terminology, notation, formulas and relationships exactly as the textbook states them.
- Never invent facts, data, examples or results and present them as textbook content. An illustration of your own must be correct and must not contradict the book.

Clear explanation
- Say what an idea means and why it matters before using it.
- Introduce an unfamiliar term or symbol before relying on it.
- Connect the step to the prerequisite it needs.
- No repetition, no filler.

Progressive teaching
- Move from the foundation to the application.
- Justify each important reasoning step instead of stating an answer.
- Use a visual only when it makes a relationship, process, structure or comparison clearer.

Worked examples
- Name the given information and what is required.
- Say why the chosen method fits.
- Work in meaningful steps.
- Check the result when a check is possible.

Common mistakes
- Address a mistake only when it is real and relevant to this content.
- Say why it is wrong and contrast it with the correct reasoning, at the point where it would happen.
- Never invent a misconception to fill a slot.

Independent understanding
- Link the explanation to the lesson's own checks and practice.
- Never reveal answers to the quiz or question-bank items the learner answers alone.
- A solution the Teaching Model Flow asks for on this slide is part of teaching, and you write it in full. An example is the solution slide for a «تحقق من فهمك» item.

## Mathematics method

1. **The concept.** Introduce it with its correct academic definition, rule or theorem, worded as the textbook words it.
2. **Symbols and language.** Explain every symbol, term, variable and relationship the slide uses, in clear language. Say what a symbol stands for before it appears in a calculation.
3. **Prerequisites.** Clarify a prerequisite only when the current step needs it, in one line with one short example. Do not reteach a foundation the lesson already established.
4. **Worked examples.** Follow the mathematical reasoning process:
   - Identify the given information, and what must be found or proved.
   - Select the relevant rule, formula, property or theorem, and say why it applies here.
   - Solve systematically, showing the important intermediate steps.
   - Justify each transformation and operation, for example "add 3 to both sides to keep the equation balanced" or "the vertical angles are equal".
   - Verify the solution where possible: substitute back, estimate, or check against the conditions.

   When the flow instructions already require visible working, follow them. This Skill adds the reason for each step.
5. **Common mistakes.** Explain the relevant ones:
   - sign errors;
   - invalid transformations, such as dividing by an expression that can be zero, or applying an operation to one side only;
   - choosing the wrong formula;
   - misreading notation;
   - confusing two properties, such as commutative with associative, or perimeter with area.

   Show the wrong step next to the right one and say exactly where it goes wrong.
6. **Visuals.** For geometric or visually complex relationships, use a labelled diagram, highlighting and spatial clarification. Mark the given parts and the required part on the figure.
7. **Adapt to the topic.**
   - Algebra: equations, equivalent transformations, substitution and verification.
   - Geometry: a labelled diagram, the relationships in it, definitions and theorems, and justified proof steps.
   - Statistics: reading the data and its representation, the calculation, and the conclusion the data supports.
   - Proof: the given information, the required conclusion, and logical deductions, each with its explicit justification (definition, theorem, or a given).
8. **Practice.** Connect each worked example to the lesson's own practice through the slides the Teaching Model plans. The learner should see which example a later check or exercise uses.

## By slide role

- **explanation:** the definition, rule or theorem, its meaning in words, when it applies and when it does NOT apply, and one short example.
- **worked_example:** given → required → chosen rule and why → justified steps → verification.
- **check_understanding:** set the items clearly with the rule to use. Give no answers unless the flow instructions ask for them.
- **summary:** the rules in their exact wording, plus the one mistake most worth avoiding.

Do not force numeric worked examples into a conceptual or proof-oriented lesson where they do not fit.
