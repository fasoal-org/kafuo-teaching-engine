# Physics Conceptual Teaching

Purpose: correct physical understanding through observation, scientific interpretation, mathematical modelling and justified application.

## How this Skill works in the lesson

- This is the PRIMARY Skill of every teaching slide at this stage. Its subject method leads how the slide teaches. A pedagogical Skill (UbD, Feynman and the like) may support it, never replace it.
- The Teaching Model Flow instructions and the slide's role (explanation, worked_example, check_understanding, summary, transition) decide which slides exist, what each slide covers, and their order. Never add, merge, split or reorder slides because of this Skill.
- The grounded textbook content is the authority for facts, definitions, notation, examples and answers. That means the cited Content Units and their book kinds: key concept, worked example, check, prior knowledge, exercise. Apply the method below only where that content supports it.
- Follow the lesson's language directive and narration rules for the spoken register.

## Shared principles

Academic accuracy
- Follow the approved curriculum content and this slide's learning objective.
- Keep definitions, terminology, notation, formulas and relationships exactly as the textbook states them.
- Never invent facts, data, examples or results and present them as textbook content. An illustration of your own must be correct and must not contradict the book.

Clear explanation
- Say what an idea means and why it matters before using it.
- Introduce an unfamiliar term or symbol before relying on it.
- Connect the step to the prerequisite it needs.
- No repetition, no filler.

Progressive teaching
- Move from the foundation to the application.
- Justify each important reasoning step instead of stating an answer.
- Use a visual only when it makes a relationship, process, structure or comparison clearer.

Worked examples
- Name the given information and what is required.
- Say why the chosen method fits.
- Work in meaningful steps.
- Check the result when a check is possible.

Common mistakes
- Address a mistake only when it is real and relevant to this content.
- Say why it is wrong and contrast it with the correct reasoning, at the point where it would happen.
- Never invent a misconception to fill a slot.

Independent understanding
- Link the explanation to the lesson's own checks and practice.
- Never reveal answers to the quiz or question-bank items the learner answers alone.
- A solution the Teaching Model Flow asks for on this slide is part of teaching, and you write it in full. An example is the solution slide for a «تحقق من فهمك» item.

## Physics method

1. **Open with the phenomenon.** Introduce the phenomenon, relationship or problem through an observable situation, or the curriculum's own illustration, when that fits the slide.
2. **Physics before mathematics.** Explain what is happening physically before any equation: what moves, what acts on what, what changes and what stays the same.
3. **Quantities.** Name the relevant quantities and variables, their symbols and SI units, and how they relate. Say which quantities are vectors and which are scalars when it matters.
4. **The law or model.** Introduce the appropriate physical law or model. Explain what it means in words, and state the conditions under which it applies, for example constant acceleration, negligible friction, or an isolated system.
5. **Quantitative examples.**
   - Identify the known and unknown quantities.
   - Select the relevant law and explain why it applies to this situation.
   - Convert units when needed.
   - Substitute the values with their units.
   - Calculate step by step.
   - Check dimensional consistency, and whether the result is physically plausible: its sign, its size and its direction.
6. **Representations.** Use diagrams, graphs, vectors (free-body diagrams), motion representations and other visuals from the curriculum when they make the situation clearer. Label quantities and directions.
7. **Misconceptions.** Explain the relevant ones, especially:
   - confusing related quantities (mass and weight, speed and velocity, heat and temperature, force and energy);
   - unit errors;
   - misreading proportional relationships ("doubling X doubles Y" only when the law says so);
   - wrong readings of motion and forces, such as "motion needs a force to continue".
8. **Connections.** Link each new concept to the principles learned before that it builds on.

## By slide role

- **explanation:** the observation, the physical meaning, the quantities with units, then the law with its conditions.
- **worked_example:** known/unknown → law and why → units → substitution with units → steps → dimensional and plausibility check.
- **check_understanding:** set the situations clearly. Give no answers unless the flow instructions ask for them.
- **summary:** the law in words and symbols, its conditions, and the one misconception most worth avoiding.

For conceptual physics lessons, put physical reasoning first and avoid calculations the lesson does not need.
