# Chemistry Scientific Teaching

Purpose: explain chemical phenomena through observable evidence, accurate scientific models, chemical representations and structured applications.

## How this Skill works in the lesson

- This is a SUPPORTING Skill. It adds the subject method to the slide's Primary Skill. It never replaces it.
- The Teaching Model Flow instructions and the slide's role (explanation, worked_example, check_understanding, summary, transition) decide which slides exist, what each slide covers, and their order. Never add, merge, split or reorder slides because of this Skill.
- The grounded textbook content is the authority for facts, definitions, notation, examples and answers. That means the cited Content Units and their book kinds: key concept, worked example, check, prior knowledge, exercise. Apply the method below only where that content supports it.
- Follow the lesson's language directive and narration rules for the spoken register.

## Shared principles

Academic accuracy
- Follow the approved curriculum content and this slide's learning objective.
- Keep definitions, terminology, notation, formulas and relationships exactly as the textbook states them.
- Never invent facts, data, examples or results and present them as textbook content. An illustration of your own must be correct and must not contradict the book.

Clear explanation
- Say what an idea means and why it matters before using it.
- Introduce an unfamiliar term or symbol before relying on it.
- Connect the step to the prerequisite it needs.
- No repetition, no filler.

Progressive teaching
- Move from the foundation to the application.
- Justify each important reasoning step instead of stating an answer.
- Use a visual only when it makes a relationship, process, structure or comparison clearer.

Worked examples
- Name the given information and what is required.
- Say why the chosen method fits.
- Work in meaningful steps.
- Check the result when a check is possible.

Common mistakes
- Address a mistake only when it is real and relevant to this content.
- Say why it is wrong and contrast it with the correct reasoning, at the point where it would happen.
- Never invent a misconception to fill a slot.

Independent understanding
- Link the explanation to the lesson's own checks and practice.
- Never reveal answers to the quiz or question-bank items the learner answers alone.
- A solution the Teaching Model Flow asks for on this slide is part of teaching, and you write it in full. An example is the solution slide for a «تحقق من فهمك» item.

## Chemistry method

1. **The subject.** Introduce the chemical phenomenon, material, reaction or concept the slide teaches.
2. **The evidence.** When it applies, describe an observable experiment, property or real-world chemical process that the textbook or another approved source supports.
3. **The mechanism.** Explain the underlying scientific mechanism, and define each term the explanation needs.
4. **Three levels.** Connect the representational levels the content uses:
   - what is observed (macroscopic behaviour);
   - what the particles or molecules are doing;
   - how symbols, formulas and equations write it.

   Say explicitly which level each statement is about.
5. **Representations.** Use diagrams, particle or molecular models, comparison tables or experimental illustrations when they make the idea clearer. Label them.
6. **Calculations and equations.**
   - Identify the given information.
   - Select the correct relationship or method, such as moles, mass, concentration or stoichiometric ratio.
   - Explain the chemical reasoning behind it.
   - Balance the equation when one is required, atom by atom.
   - Show the substitutions and calculations step by step with units.
   - Check the units, conservation of mass and atoms, and whether the result is reasonable.
7. **Experiments.** Explain the purpose, the observations, their interpretation and the conclusion. Report only the observations and results the source gives, and never invent experimental results.
8. **Misconceptions.** Explain the relevant ones, especially:
   - confusing physical and chemical changes;
   - wrong formulas or charges;
   - balancing by changing subscripts instead of coefficients;
   - wrong particle representations;
   - unit-conversion errors.
9. **Comparisons.** Use a comparison when it separates related substances, processes or concepts, such as an element and a compound, a mixture and a compound, or an exothermic and an endothermic process.

## By slide role

- **explanation:** the phenomenon, the evidence, then the particle-level explanation, then the symbols, with each term defined.
- **worked_example:** given → method and why → balanced equation if needed → steps with units → conservation and reasonableness check.
- **check_understanding:** set the items clearly. Give no answers unless the flow instructions ask for them.
- **summary:** the key relationship or equation, what it means at the particle level, and the one misconception most worth avoiding.

Do not require experiments or calculations in lessons where they do not apply.
